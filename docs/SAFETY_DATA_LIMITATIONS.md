# RockMap Safety & Data Limitations

**Disclosure version 1 · August 19, 2026**

RockMap is an informational reference and planning tool. It is NOT intended to validate, certify, survey, prove, or guarantee the accuracy of any location, mineral deposit, mine, land-management boundary, property boundary, mining-claim boundary, road, trail, water feature, access route, collecting area, or other mapped or searched feature.

RockMap uses publicly available and publicly distributed source data, then transforms portions of that data into compact offline map and search products. RockMap does not independently verify the truth, completeness, age, legal status, field condition, coordinate precision, or positional accuracy of those source records. Public datasets can contain errors, stale information, generalized geometry, approximate points, incomplete records, missing features, mismatched names, outdated status, or locations that differ materially from conditions on the ground.

DO NOT use RockMap to:
- survey or establish property, land-management, or mining-claim boundaries;
- stake, monument, file, or precisely plot a mining claim or other legal interest;
- determine whether land is public, private, open, closed, claimed, unclaimed, or legally accessible;
- determine whether collecting, prospecting, digging, driving, parking, camping, or entering an area is lawful;
- prove that a mineral deposit, specimen locality, mine, road, trail, water source, or other feature exists at the displayed location;
- navigate where an inaccurate map, missing road, wrong coordinate, or stale condition could create a safety risk;
- replace current official records, surveyed coordinates, posted signs, landowner permission, agency guidance, legal research, emergency information, or field verification.

Coordinates shown by RockMap are reference coordinates only. Even when a source provides precise-looking decimal coordinates, that does not mean the underlying record was surveyed or is accurate to that precision. GPS positions from the phone also have measurement error and can be affected by terrain, sky view, hardware, atmosphere, multipath, and Android location behavior.


## Public Source Families Used By Rockmap

Depending on the installed RockMap data pack and feature, RockMap uses or derives information from:
- OpenStreetMap contributors, delivered through Protomaps-derived basemap data;
- U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) National Map / GNIS-style geographic-name and Gazetteer data;
- Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT) state-highway data;
- Bureau of Land Management (BLM) Colorado Surface Management Agency data;
- BLM Mineral & Land Records System (MLRS), including Mining Claims — Not Closed records;
- USGS Mineral Resources Data System (MRDS);
- USGS MAS/MILS data, including Colorado records published through USGS Open-File Report 03-090;
- Colorado Geological Survey (CGS) radioactive-mineral occurrence data derived from Bulletin 40 / ON-B-40D;
- CGS nonmetallic and industrial-mineral mine inventory data, including MS-17 / IS-62 lineage;
- CGS / U.S. Forest Service abandoned-mine inventory data, including ON-008-04D;
- CGS historic metal-mining district data, including ON-007-08D;
- reviewed official CGS and USGS gemstone / mineral-locality references included in RockMap's compact offline evidence sets.

Source coverage changes over time. A source being listed here does not mean every RockMap build contains every source, nor does it make the source authoritative for a particular legal, surveying, navigation, or field-safety purpose.


## Land And Claim Data

BLM Surface Management Agency mapping is management/status context, not a parcel survey or legal boundary. RockMap's mining-claim overlay uses BLM MLRS records selected from a Not Closed dataset; it is not a surveyed claim-boundary product. Some records may have no geospatial representation, may use generalized geometry, or may not reflect later filings or field conditions. A blank map result is UNKNOWN, not proof that land is public, unclaimed, open, or lawful to enter.


## Mineral And Mine Data

Mineral, mine, district, commodity, and locality records are research evidence only. A record does not prove that a collectible specimen or economic deposit exists at the displayed point today. Historic mine locations and status may be approximate or outdated. District boundaries are broad and subjective. A displayed representative point may stand for an area rather than an exact occurrence.


## User Responsibility

Before acting on RockMap information, verify important facts with the current responsible agency, county or landowner records, official BLM/USGS/CGS/CDOT sources as appropriate, surveyed or professionally verified coordinates when precision matters, posted signs and closures, and direct field observation. For safety-critical navigation, use a navigation product and source appropriate to that purpose and carry appropriate backup navigation.

By continuing, you acknowledge that RockMap is a reference tool with these limitations and that displayed information may be incomplete, outdated, approximate, or wrong.
